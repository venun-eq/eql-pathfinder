Upgrades
What to raise
Working it out…

A mote or fragment goes into items up to one tier below its own (a Fragment of Grand fits up to +7); spells have no limit. Fragments condense 2:1 like motes, so a fragment is worth most condensed up and put into your highest gear: the plan uses them on +8 → +9 when a Void-Touched Potential does +9 → +10, and on +9 → +10 when none does. It doesn't spend them on spells or lower gear. Motes are counted at their T5-eq and fragments at that best use. Points past the goal carry into the next tier. The numbers come from the in-game preview (docs: motes and fragments).

How Pathfinder counts

The rules behind every number in the app.

Your data
What it reads and sends
Reads, on this PC
  • Your EverQuest Legends log (type /log on in game once).
  • Your inventory export (/outputfile inventory), for Upgrades.
  • The game's spell file, to tell which class cast what.

It never reads game memory, never changes game files and never plays for you.

Sends
  • Your play, as numbers, only if you turn on sharing. shows exactly what goes.
  • Feedback and reports, only when you press Send. A report's screenshot shows Pathfinder's page alone, with your character's name, clock times and folders hidden, and you see it first.
  • Otherwise it only downloads: updates, the numbers players share, and What's coming.
Numbers from other players
  • Rebuilt every 30 minutes from the crawls and hunts of players who share. No character names, no chat, no clock times.
  • A number shows only once enough players and crawls are behind it. Ask says how many.
  • One very active player can't drown out the rest: each player's weight in a number is capped.
  • Everyone gets them, whether or not they share.
  • Leaving sharing stops new uploads; can also delete everything you sent.
Time
Crawls and active time

Every "per hour" in Pathfinder divides by active time: the gold below. Grey isn't counted.

A crawl
  • Starts when you zone into a difficulty instance ("… 4 (Refined)").
  • Ends when the Dungeon Crawl completes, or when you leave a normal instance.
  • Leave and come back within 10 minutes and it's the same crawl, unless you made a new instance. Time outside isn't counted.
  • Kills after the crawl completes still count, and so does their time.
Relevels
  • A relevel is a trio's crawls and open-world hunting from its reincarnation until it reaches the cap.
  • Its hours are active time, so travel isn't counted.
  • Ask scales older visits to how fast you play now: gear, upgrades and AAs make you faster month by month (August visits ran about half again as long as November's), so a place last played months ago isn't predicted at an old pace. Ask says how much under each answer.
  • The race (Home while you relevel, and History → Relevels) shows your best relevel, the middle half of them as a band and the typical one dashed. Your relevel in progress is gold; its dashed part is the rest of the way at your finished trios' pace, adjusted by how this trio has gone so far.
  • Each reincarnation rank makes every level need 10% more XP (mobs give the same XP; the bar is bigger), so relevels at different ranks compare at the same XP: your best is scaled to what the trio you're on, or the next one, needs.
  • Home follows what you're doing: a relevel in progress, farming at the cap (a crawl at the cap in the last 2 hours), or between relevels. At the cap you can switch it.
Am I going at a proper speed?

Levels an hour can't say on its own: it falls every level, and your reincarnation XP, a group or a different place move it too. So Pathfinder splits it into the part you control and the part the place decides, and compares each like for like. Your numbers are always shown as they happened; only the comparison adjusts.

Kill speed is against your usual at the same place and difficulty, within 2 levels, solo or grouped the same, from 3 or more runs. Your reincarnation XP doesn't change it. Motes, fragments and T5-equivalent

T5-equivalent (T5-eq) puts every mote on one scale: spell points ÷ 16. A Major mote is 1, and each tier is worth twice the one below.

Points are what one mote or fragment adds. A few of the smallest and rarest values follow the pattern rather than an in-game check.

The rules
  • Going from +t to +t+1 costs 2t points, on items and spells: +4 → +5 costs 16, +9 → +10 costs 512.
  • On a spell, a fragment adds what one mote of the next tier does: 2 same-tier motes. On gear it beats a mote by more the higher the item, up to 4.5 at +9 → +10, and fragments condense 2:1, so they belong on your highest gear. T5-eq counts a fragment at the value you pick in ; the next card, , says which to pick.
  • Condensing at Constructed Potential turns 2 motes into 1 of the next tier. Nothing is lost on spells, a lot on gear: 2 Majors are 10 gear points, the Greater they make is 6. Fragments condense the same way (since the 11-17 patch).
  • Reward Chest motes count.
The upgrade plan
  • The cheapest mix of your motes, your fragments and Majors still to farm.
  • A mote costs its T5-eq and a Void-Touched Potential takes a whole top tier. A fragment costs its best use up to +9, the recommendation: condensed to a Fragment of Ascendant in a +8 item. So the plan keeps fragments for your highest gear: +8 → +9, or +9 → +10 when no VTP does that tier.
  • Your mote counts come from you (Upgrades): the log shows loot and condensing, but not motes going into items or spells.
What's a fragment worth?

Fragments condense 2:1 like motes: 32 Major fragments make 1 Fragment of Infinite, just as 32 Major motes make 1 Infinite mote. From the same 32, the fragment gives far more:

Planning
How you play and the farm rate

Pathfinder judges a visit at the difficulties you farm () by T5-eq per hour against your usual there, and the rest as leveling, by levels per hour. Grouped play, instances you never finished and play at the level cap are always judged by T5-eq per hour. On Ask, Rank by decides what "best" means:

The farm rate
  • What an hour at the cap is worth in motes, used to weigh levels against motes.
  • Taken from your own crawls at the cap since the 11-10 patch, at the difficulties you farm for motes (; D3 and D4 unless you change them): your best place or your typical, once three of them have a rate.
  • Until then, or if you prefer, a rate you type yourself: pick Set it under . Without either, a default of 50 T5-eq/h. Ask and Upgrades say which one they use.
Your loadouts and /who

Pathfinder follows your loadout swaps within seconds, from the files the game saves for each loadout.

  • The first time you're on a loadout, type /who once. It names the classes, race and level, and Pathfinder remembers them.
  • Changed a class on a loadout? /who once more: the game's files show that a class changed, not which.
  • Reincarnating keeps the loadout you're on and only sets the level back to 1, so it needs no /who.
  • Until the /who, Home asks for it, and the overlay and the fight meter say the loadout isn't known rather than compare you with another loadout's usual.
Which loadout you're on never leaves this PC.
Your relevels, like-for-like

History → Relevels compares the relevels you played, never trios you haven't.

  1. Pace: each relevel's solo crawls, in levels per hour, against your other relevels in the same zone, difficulty and 5-level band, with the reincarnation penalty taken out. A little overlap is pulled toward your usual.
  2. Where the hours went: the stretches that took longest past your usual hours for those levels.

Pathfinder doesn't forecast hours for a trio. How fast one levels depends on what each class does with the other two, so a trio you haven't played can't be measured from the ones you have. Ask's hours by place come from crawls, yours and the community's.

The campaign
  • Plan → Campaign plans every reincarnation you mean to do. Click a class's or race's pips for the rank you want (up to 3); click your goal's pip again to lower it. A loadout is a race and three classes, run 1 to 3 times.
  • It plans the cheapest way from your ranks now: the fewest runs, then the least XP to level (a relevel needs the XP of its highest rank, so even ranks are the cheapest), then your classes first, since Completionist counts only classes. Between plans that cost the same, each trio holds up where you level and gets a carry from Plan → Class roles, best first.
  • It says whether your plan is the cheapest, with the arithmetic: a run earns one race rank and three class ranks, so "24 class ranks to earn" is at least 8 runs. When a plan misses a goal or has runs to spare, it offers fixes, cheapest first.
  • Your Must combos and anything set to Unlock now come first, at the price they show. Everything else only where it's free.
  • Every class and race passive tops out at 3, and Completionist goes up when every class reaches 1, 2 and 3. Every class 3/3 takes 16 runs from scratch; everything 3/3 takes 48, because each race needs three runs of its own. Those extra runs raise classes past 3, which adds XP to level and no passive.
  • Classes come in rounds: every class to 1, then to 2, then to 3, so Completionist comes as soon as it can. A class or race you rank under Classes you want first or Races you want first is earned first wherever that's free; Unlock now plays its runs back to back, at the price shown. Races still short come after, one loadout each.
  • A run is done when its loadout is back at the cap after its reincarnation. Until then it reads leveling, with its level: you can have several leveling at once, say to spend a crawl token on a parked loadout. One the plan doesn't have is shown, and "Rebuild the rest" plans from where you are, keeping pinned loadouts and the runs already done.
  • Swap… on a loadout shows the cheapest changes that keep every goal, or lets you pick a race or class yourself: each choice says what it does, runs first, then XP. The ⋯ menu sets how many times a loadout runs, moves it, or removes it.
  • Reincarnated while your log was off? "Mark a run done" in its loadout's ⋯ menu.
Reincarnation counts
  • The log says "Class Reincarnation: Warrior" with no number, so Pathfinder counts the ones it sees.
  • Any from while your log was off are invisible to it. Set them under (Set counts…).
  • A count you set is your total as of that moment. Ones the log shows afterwards add to it.
  • Re-reading the logs keeps your counts.
Fights and what's left out
DPS and kill time
  • DPS counts your melee, spells, DoTs, damage shield and your pet.
  • History's DPS is damage over all your time inside. Ask's damage comparison counts only the seconds you were fighting, and the fight meter can use either (, DPS counts).
  • A charmed pet counts as yours only while the charm holds, and only on what it was sent at or what you're fighting. Damage to yourself never counts.
  • Kill time is the median from your first hit on a mob to its death.
What's left out
  • DDD before the patch: crawls from before the 11-10 patch where Denon's Desperate Dirge did most of the damage count for nothing: not in routes, farm rates, paces, Ask or the shared numbers. DDD since the nerf counts like any other spell.
  • Bard hunts before the patch: open-world hunts from before it on a bard (or unknown) trio, since damage outside instances isn't kept.
  • Planes below 46: Hate, Fear and Sky open at 46, so Ask never offers them lower. A plane visit your history puts below 46 was a loadout at the cap you'd swapped to without a /who since, so its level is the last one Pathfinder saw: Ask leaves it out.
  • Idle and travel: quiet time past 3 minutes inside, and gaps over 4 minutes outside (the diagram under Time).
